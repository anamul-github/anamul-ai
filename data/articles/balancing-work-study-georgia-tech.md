# Balancing Work and Study: Why I Started My Master's at Georgia Tech?

By Anamul Hasan, published 23 March, 2025. Category: Career 💼, Learning 🏫

##### Introduction:

Why would someone already in the tech industry go back to school?

It’s a question I kept asking myself before enrolling at Georgia Tech. After all, I already had a tech job, years of hands-on experience, and the flexibility to learn almost anything online. But something was still missing—a desire to dive deeper, build stronger foundations, and challenge myself intellectually.

In this article, I'll explain why I chose to pursue a master's degree at Georgia Tech and how formal education provided structure, mentorship, and growth opportunities that self-learning cannot offer.

##### The Power of Structured Learning

Nowadays, information is at our fingertips. We can learn almost anything online. Videos, blogs, and courses are just a click away. I used to learn this way too—whenever and however I wanted. But I found that **self-learning**, while flexible, often lacks direction. There’s no one to guide you, no team to learn with, no real deadlines to push you. Without that structure, I didn’t feel the pressure to give my best.

Returning to school at Georgia Tech offered exactly what I needed: structured learning, regular deadlines, collaborative group projects, and access to mentors. It provided the environment that motivates me to perform at my highest level, where I can grow by pushing myself beyond my comfort zone.

Ultimately, it wasn't just about gaining knowledge. It was about joining a learning journey toward continuous growth, staying accountable, connecting with like-minded professionals, and preparing for the challenges ahead in tech.

##### What Self-Learning Can’t Always Teach

For a long time, I believed traditional education wasn’t necessary for success. In many cases, that’s still true. Plenty of people thrive by teaching themselves and learning through experience. I used to think that if I needed a skill, I could simply train myself to become good at it.

But over time, I realized that self-learning has its limits. I had underestimated the value of **learning from others—one of the most powerful ways to grow**. Being part of a structured environment with mentors, peers, and hands-on projects adds a whole new layer to the learning process. It makes things more practical and more applicable. Collaborative learning forces you to think differently, solve problems together, and build something meaningful—just like in the real world. That’s what makes traditional learning so valuable.

##### Why I Chose Georgia Tech

While searching for a graduate program that would align with my full-time work schedule, I considered a few top universities offering online options. I narrowed it down to two options: ***Harvard University*** and ***Georgia Institute of Technology***. After careful research, I chose Georgia Tech for several important reasons.

First and foremost, Georgia Tech has a strong global reputation in **Computer Science, research, and innovation**. The Online Master of Science in Computer Science (OMSCS) program is not only one of the most respected in the field, but also one of the most affordable—making world-class education accessible without sacrificing quality. That combination of **prestige and affordability** was hard to beat.

What impressed me even more was Georgia Tech’s commitment to advancing technology. The university consistently ranks among the top in areas like ***Artificial Intelligence, Cybersecurity, Machine Learning*, and *Systems*** research. The faculty includes leading experts in the field, and the curriculum is built to reflect real-world demands. It’s not just academic theory—it’s applicable, hands-on learning at the highest level.

##### A Flexible and Strategic Fit

Since I work full time, I needed a program that could fit into my schedule but still push me intellectually. Georgia Tech offered exactly that. It gave me the chance to learn at my own pace, collaborate with talented peers from around the world, and gain experience that directly applies to my work and long-term goals.

**Choosing Georgia Tech wasn’t just a practical decision—it was a strategic one.** It gave me the opportunity to learn, grow, contribute, and be part of a forward-looking tech community.

##### Balancing Work and Study

This is the first time I am juggling both a full-time job and full-time graduate studies. To be honest, it’s been one of the hardest challenges I have faced. Some days feel like a marathon, switching between work and coursework without much breathing room. I often find myself studying late at night or catching up on weekends, or even using PTO (personal time off) just to stay on track. Free time is rare, and there is always something that needs my attention.

Still, I remind myself why I started. I try to stay organized using calendar apps and to-do lists, and I have learned to prioritize the most important tasks each day. It’s not easy, and I am still figuring it out, but **every small step forward feels like progress**. Balancing both commitments has taught me discipline, focus, and the importance of managing energy, not just time. And even in the struggle, I know this journey is shaping me into a stronger, more resilient version of myself.

##### Conclusion

Going back to school while working full time hasn’t been easy, but it’s been one of the best decisions I have made. This journey pushed me out of my comfort zone, reminded me of the value of structured learning, and showed me how important community and mentorship really are.

Georgia Tech gave me more than just a degree path—it gave me **direction and purpose**. While the challenges haven’t gone away, I feel more prepared to face them. If you’re thinking about going back to school while working full time, know this: **it’s hard, but it’s worth it**.
